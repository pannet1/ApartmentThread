Stage 3b — Door Contact Sensor Bench Build (DS-1)

Assembling and wiring the hardwired MC-38 magnetic reed switch door contact sensor into the Tinxy switch module's S2 dummy channel input. This is the immediate build priority — runs 100% standalone on the Tinxy on-chip state machine with no Home Assistant server required. The alternative wireless Matter-over-Thread build (Track B) is documented below for layouts where the switchboard is distant from the door.

Stage 3b Objective

Build and verify the DS-1 Door Contact Sensor. In Indian apartments where the bathroom switchboard is directly adjacent to the door frame, the default approach is the Hardwired Dummy Channel: a reed switch connected into switch input S2 of the in-wall Tinxy module (O-12, O-13). No firmware changes needed: it works out of the box with the stock Tinxy mobile app (using Routines and auto-off timers). For advanced users who want custom offline state machines, flashing ESPHome is optional.

1. Sensor Architecture: Hardwired Dummy Channel vs Wireless

Because the bathroom door serves as an absolute physical boundary, detecting door transitions enables instant lighting and continuous occupancy locking:

  • Track A (Default — Hardwired Dummy Channel): Senses door state via a dry contact reed switch wired straight into the bathroom switchboard's S2 input. Works with stock factory firmware via the Tinxy mobile app (Routines & Auto-off timer) — zero flashing, zero server required. (Optional: custom ESPHome can be flashed if you want complex on-chip state machines without the Tinxy app).
  • Track B (Wireless — Matter over Thread): Uses a Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32-C6 running as a Thread Sleepy End Device (<5μA deep sleep, 4–5 year LiPo life) joining the central M5Stack ESP32-H2 border router mesh when running conduit to the switchboard is impractical.

2. Direct Interconnect & Wiring Schemes (O-13)

Select the wiring scheme matching your bathroom layout:

Track A: Hardwired Dummy Channel Wiring (Tinxy In-Wall Module)

A1. Hardware: Bare Glass Reed Switch vs MC-38 Kit

Whether you bought a complete MC-38 kit or just a bare glass reed switch (looks like a miniature glass fuse with two iron wire leads):

  • Bare Glass Reed Switch: A sealed glass capsule with two nickel/iron leads. You solder one insulated wire to each lead, insulate them with heat-shrink, and place the delicate tube inside the TicTac plastic shell.
  • The Magnet: If using a bare reed switch, use any small magnet (a ₹20 neodymium disc magnet, a craft magnet, or a strong fridge magnet) mounted on the moving door leaf.
  • No Polarity (+ve / −ve): A reed switch is purely mechanical dry contacts. There is no positive or negative. The two wires connect to the switch terminals in any order.
Pure Dry Contact — Zero Polarity

Just like a standard mechanical wall light switch, a reed switch simply makes or breaks an electrical circuit when a magnetic field is applied. Connect wire 1 to S2 and wire 2 to COM in any order.

A2. Preparing & Soldering the Bare Reed Switch (TicTac Shell)

Because the glass capsule is fragile, take care when soldering and mounting:

  1. Heat-Sink While Soldering: Grip the metal lead with needle-nose pliers or tweezers between the solder point and the glass body. The pliers absorb heat so excessive soldering iron heat does not crack the airtight glass-to-metal seal.
  2. Solder Two Wires: Solder one thin insulated wire (0.5 mm²) to each lead. Slip heat-shrink tubing over both solder joints to prevent them from shorting together.
  3. Prepare the TicTac Shell:
    • Drill or melt two tiny 2 mm holes in the bottom or side of a cleaned, empty TicTac box.
    • Pass the two wires through the holes.
    • Cushion the glass tube inside using a drop of hot glue, double-sided foam tape, or a piece of cotton/foam so door vibrations do not shatter the glass.
    • Snap the TicTac lid shut. The hard plastic shell protects the glass from accidental impacts.
  4. Mount to Door Frame: Stick the TicTac box to the exterior door frame header or strike jamb using 3M VHB double-sided tape.
Caution — Fragile Glass

Never bend the iron leads directly flush against the glass tube; always hold the lead with pliers and bend beyond the pliers. Cracking the glass allows air in, causing the switch contacts to oxidize and fail.

A3. Magnet Selection & Placement (Moving Door)

  • What Magnet to Use: Any small neodymium magnet (e.g. 10×2 mm or 12×3 mm disc), a furniture cabinet magnetic catch, or a fridge magnet.
  • Mounting: Mount the magnet on the moving door leaf directly facing the glass reed switch inside the TicTac box.
  • Operating Gap: Set the gap to 5–10 mm when the door is fully shut. When closed, the magnetic field pulls the internal reeds together (circuit closed). When the door swings open, the reeds separate (circuit open).

A4. Wiring to the Tinxy Module Inside the Switchboard

The two wires coming out of the TicTac box route into the bathroom switchboard box and connect across the Tinxy module’s S2 input:

Wiring Schematic — Just Two Wires into S2 
  DOOR FRAME (TicTac Box)         TINXY MODULE (Inside Switchboard)
  ======================         =================================

  [Bare Reed Switch]
     Lead 1 + Wire 1  ———————→  S2 (Switch 2 terminal)
     Lead 2 + Wire 2  ———————→  COM / Switch Common terminal

                                 CHANNEL 1 — Bathroom Light:
                                   L1 → Bathroom light bulb
                                   S1 ← Manual wall rocker switch

                                 CHANNEL 2 — Dummy Channel:
                                   L2 → LEAVE UNCONNECTED (empty)

  * Polarity does not matter: Wire 1 and Wire 2 are completely interchangeable.
  * Channel 2 relay output (L2) stays empty so S2 acts as an on-chip sensor input.
  1. Channel 1 (Light & Fan): L1 powers the bathroom light fixture. S1 is connected to the manual wall rocker switch on the faceplate (manual override always functional).
  2. Channel 2 (Dummy Door Trigger): L2 is left empty. The two wires from your reed switch connect to S2 and its common reference terminal.
Mains Safety

The Tinxy module is powered by 230V AC mains. Ensure the mains MCB is switched OFF before opening the switchboard. Connect the two low-voltage reed switch wires securely into screw terminals or Wago connectors.

A5. Bench Test

  1. Multimeter continuity: Touch the two MC-38 wires. Hold magnet near → beep (closed). Pull magnet away → no beep (open).
  2. Tinxy app test: With the module powered and reed switch wired to S2, toggle the magnet. Channel 2 should change state in the Tinxy app.
  3. Gap check: Find the maximum working gap. Aim for 5–10 mm installed.

A6. Final Door Frame Mounting

  1. Mount the reed switch (in TicTac shell or original MC-38 housing) on the exterior door frame using VHB tape or screws.
  2. Mount the magnet on the moving door leaf, aligned with the reed switch, 5–10 mm gap when closed.
  3. Route the wires along the door frame into the switchboard box.
  4. Open and close the door several times — confirm Tinxy app shows Channel 2 toggling.

3. Flashing Matter-over-Thread Firmware

The Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32-C6 runs open-source Matter-over-Thread Contact Sensor firmware based on Espressif ESP-Matter and the ESP-IDF framework.

A. Toolchain Setup & Repository Clone

# 1. Install prerequisites
sudo apt-get update && sudo apt-get install -y git python3-pip cmake ninja-build

# 2. Clone ESP-Matter with submodules
mkdir -p ~/esp && cd ~/esp
git clone --depth 1 --recursive https://github.com/espressif/esp-matter.git
cd esp-matter
./install.sh

B. Build & Flash via USB-C

Connect the XIAO ESP32-C6 to your PC via USB-C, identify its port (typically /dev/ttyACM0 or /dev/ttyUSB0), and compile the contact sensor example targeting Thread:

cd ~/esp/esp-matter/examples/contact_sensor
source ~/esp/esp-matter/export.sh

# Configure target for ESP32-C6 and Thread
idf.py set-target esp32c6

# Build and flash
idf.py -p /dev/ttyACM0 build flash monitor
Pre-Built Binary Option Available

If you prefer not to compile ESP-IDF from source on your workstation, you can flash the pre-compiled binary directly using esptool.py:

pip install --user esptool
esptool.py -p /dev/ttyACM0 -b 460800 --chip esp32c6 write_flash 0x0 ds1_matter_thread.bin

C. Extract Onboarding Codes

During the first boot in the monitor terminal, the firmware outputs the standard Matter pairing discriminator, QR code URL, and manual pairing code:

I (892) chip[DL]: Setup Discriminator: 3840 (0xf00)
I (895) chip[DL]: Setup Passcode: 20202021
I (901) chip[SVR]: Manual pairing code: [34970112332]
I (908) chip[SVR]: Setup QRCode: [MT:Y.K90-Q000KA0648G00]

4. Commissioning into Home Assistant

Commissioning the door sensor follows the same local-first flow as the presence sensor:

  1. Keep Sensor in Direct Line-of-Sight: For initial Thread pairing, place the XIAO ESP32-C6 within 1–2 meters of the Radxa server / ESP32-H2 border router.
  2. Open Home Assistant Companion App: Ensure your phone is connected to the local home Wi-Fi network.
  3. Add Matter Device: In Home Assistant, go to Settings → Devices & Services → Add Integration → Add Matter Device.
  4. Scan QR or Enter Code: Enter the manual pairing code from the firmware build (e.g. 34970112332).
  5. Assign Area: Name the entity `Bathroom Door Sensor` and assign it to the Bathroom area.
  6. Verify State: Home Assistant will create binary_sensor.bathroom_door under the Matter integration.

5. Bench Testing & Latency Verification

Before final adhesive mounting on the door frame, run this 3-step bench test to prove latency and contact behavior:

Bench Test Sequence
  1. Closed State Test (Magnet In Contact): Hold the magnet piece directly against the MC-38 sensor body (gap <10 mm). Confirm that binary_sensor.bathroom_door in Home Assistant shows `Off` (Closed).
  2. Open State Test (Magnet Removed): Pull the magnet away (>20 mm). Confirm that the state flips immediately to `On` (Open) in under 50 ms. closes O-12
  3. Rapid Toggle Stress Test: Move the magnet back and forth rapidly 5 times. Confirm that Home Assistant records every single transition without dropping packets or stalling the Thread border router.

6. Physical Enclosure & Door Frame Mounting

Once bench testing passes, mount the door sensor on the bathroom door following these architectural guidelines:

  • Exterior Frame Location: Mount the sensor body (with XIAO and battery) on the outside header jamb or exterior door frame. This keeps the electronics 100% protected from shower steam and condensation.
  • Magnet Orientation: Affix the magnet piece to the moving door leaf directly aligned with the sensor body marker notch. Ensure the gap when the door is closed is 5–10 mm (well within the rated 15 mm maximum).
  • Mounting Medium: Use outdoor-rated 3M VHB double-sided acrylic foam tape (BOM Stage 5) for clean, vibration-proof attachment that resists door slams.
Pass — Stage 3b Door Sensor Gate

The DS-1 Door Contact Sensor is fully verified on the bench: reports open/close transitions across the Thread mesh within 50 ms, maintains <5μA deep sleep current, and operates 100% locally with zero cloud dependencies.
