Presence Sensor

The battery device that tells Home Assistant a room is occupied. Built on the open-source Sentinel reference design (Localized Tech): HLK-LD2410S 24 GHz radar, Seeed Studio XIAO nRF52840, and native Matter-over-Thread with 4–5 months proven battery life.

Component identity — PS-1

Every presence sensor in this build is identified as PS-1: HLK-LD2410S radar + Seeed Studio XIAO nRF52840 + 2,000 mAh LiPo cell + Sentinel firmware (Matter over Thread) in an articulating ball-and-socket enclosure. Commissions natively via standard Matter QR code directly into Home Assistant — zero cloud dependency, no proprietary hubs.

PCB Architecture, Pinout & Trace Details

The presence sensor utilizes a dedicated 2-layer printed circuit board designed to fit inside the articulating 3D-printed ball mount. It eliminates point-to-point wiring loops that degrade the 24 GHz microwave radar signal and provides hardware-level power isolation between the radar RF frontend and the nRF52840 2.4 GHz Thread mesh radio.

Circuit Design Highlights
  • Full Hardware Power Gating: The ST LDLN030G33R LDO regulator enables the XIAO microcontroller to physically de-energize the HLK-LD2410S radar during deep sleep intervals via GPIO pin D2 (LDO_EN), collapsing idle draw from ~600 µA to <4 µA and unlocking 4–5 months of real-world battery life on a 2,000 mAh LiPo.
  • Castellated Pad & Underside Window Routing: The Seeed Studio XIAO nRF52840 exposes its LiPo battery charger pins (BAT+ and BAT-) and SWD debugging pins exclusively on the bottom face of the module. The Sentinel PCB incorporates through-milled plated via windows (pads 15–16 and 19–22), enabling direct solder bridging from the reverse side into the XIAO's onboard Texas Instruments BQ25100 charging IC.
  • Dedicated Ultra-Low-Noise Rail: The LD2410S radar requires an exceptionally clean 3.3V rail. Driving the radar directly from digital MCU outputs or noisy switching supplies causes false ghost reflections. The LDLN030G33R delivers a ripple noise of just 7.5 µVRMS with local LC filtering.

Hardware Engineering: Why Radar Power Gating is Critical

Standard ESP32 or mmWave builds power radar modules continuously. The Hi-Link HLK-LD2410S draws approximately 0.5 mA to 0.6 mA in active detection mode. While minimal compared to older 24 GHz modules (which drew 50–80 mA), leaving it powered 24/7 on a 2,000 mAh battery depletes the cell in under 18 weeks without accounting for MCU radio transmissions:

Operational TopologyRadar Active StateMicrocontroller StateAverage CurrentBattery Life (2,000 mAh LiPo)
No Power Gating (Constant Radar)Powered ON 24/7 (0.6 mA)Thread Sleepy End Device (ICD)~620 µA~4.4 months maximum
Sentinel Dynamic Power Gating (PS-1)Gated OFF via D2 in quiet periods; ON during presence confirmationNordic nRF52840 Deep Sleep (3 µA)~18–45 µA avg4–5+ months proven in active homes

Furthermore, running the radar module directly off the Seeed Studio XIAO's integrated 3.3V LDO creates RF cross-talk: the 802.15.4 Thread transceiver bursts at +8 dBm, causing voltage transients that introduce false motion triggers on the sensitive microwave analog mixer. The dedicated ST LDLN030G33R regulator and decoupling capacitors (C1–C4) isolate the two RF domains completely.

Sentinel Circuit Engineering: ST LDLN030G33R & Filtering Passives

The Sentinel reference PCB incorporates a dedicated power-gating and decoupling network around the ST LDLN030G33R ultra-low-noise LDO. Every passive component plays a mandatory role in maintaining radar accuracy and multi-month battery life:

Circuit Component Functions
  • LDO Negative Feedback Loop Stability (C2 & C3): The ST LDLN030G33R requires an output capacitance (COUT ≥ 1 µF) to maintain closed-loop phase margin. The reference design places C2 (4.7 µF ceramic) and C3 (0.1 µF high-frequency bypass) directly at the regulator output pins to prevent loop oscillation and deliver pristine 3.3V DC with only 7.5 µVRMS residual noise.
  • Radar FMCW Chirp Current Transient Absorption: While the HLK-LD2410S averages ~0.6 mA, its 24 GHz microwave frequency synthesizer draws sharp microsecond current spikes during frequency sweeps. Local output capacitors C2 and C3 supply these instantaneous bursts, preventing the 3.3V supply rail from dipping below the radar's 3.0V brownout threshold and eliminating phantom occupancy triggers.
  • Thread Radio Burst Isolation (C1 & C4): When the nRF52840's 2.4 GHz Thread radio fires +8 dBm mesh packet transmissions, it draws transient current pulses of 15–20 mA from the battery rail. Input capacitors C1 (10 µF) and C4 (0.1 µF) absorb these spikes on the +BATT rail, preventing RF switching noise from leaking into the radar frontend.
  • Floating Enable Gate Pull-Down (R1): When the XIAO nRF52840 is in deep sleep or bootloader reset, its GPIO pins enter a high-impedance (tri-state) state. Resistor R1 (100 kΩ pull-down to GND) keeps the LDO enable pin tied low, ensuring the radar power rail stays completely OFF and preventing battery drain during sleep.
