Local-Only Smart Calling Bell

A plug-and-play, zero-cloud calling bell for the Indian market: custom WAV/MP3 chimes uploaded over a local Wi-Fi web interface into LittleFS, an hourly / on-demand NTP-synchronised talking clock with RTC backup, and scheduled voice reminders. This page is a standalone hardware guide — it deliberately sits outside the five-section Thread/Matter navigation chain so the main wiki's strict reading order stays intact.

Scope & Conventions

This is a separate product document, not a new section of the Apartment Thread blueprint. It reuses the same shell, badges, tables, and verification-gate conventions as the rest of the wiki. Pin numbers, prices, and gain straps are engineering targets for a first bench prototype; confirm them against the current datasheet and the cart before committing to a PCB.

1 — Circuit & Pin-to-Pin Wiring Guide

Target: ESP32-S3-N16R8 (DevKit for the prototype, bare ESP32-S3-WROOM-1-N16R8 for the production board). The 16 MB flash is not optional — the chime files live in a LittleFS partition on that flash, and the 8 MB PSRAM gives the MP3 decoder room to buffer.

Strapping & Reserved Pins — Read Before Wiring

Avoid GPIO0, GPIO3, GPIO45 and GPIO46 on the ESP32-S3: these are strapping pins and a stray connection here changes boot behaviour. On the WROOM-1-N16R8 module, GPIO26–32 are consumed by the on-module flash/PSRAM and GPIO33–37 are not broken out; GPIO43/44 are UART0 and GPIO19/20 are the native USB data pair. Note that GPIO15 is safe on the S3 even though it is a strapping pin on the original ESP32. Every GPIO chosen below (4–8, 14–16) is outside all of those groups.

MAX98357A GAIN Strap — Verify Against Your Datasheet Revision

The GAIN pin sets analog gain by its DC voltage, not by firmware. The safe starting point is GAIN tied directly to GND (lowest gain, roughly 3 dB), which avoids clipping if an uploaded WAV is hot. Intermediate resistor-to-GND values and tying GAIN to VIN step the gain up. Confirm the exact resistor table in the Maxim/ADI MAX98357A datasheet before ordering the resistor. The SD pin is the one you actually control from firmware (GPIO7).

Why 5 V / 2 A (and Where It Gets Spent)

MAX98357A delivers about 3 W into 4 Ω only when its VIN is 5 V — feeding it from 3V3 roughly quarters the output power. Peak draw is roughly 1.2 A for the amplifier at full swing plus ~0.5 A for ESP32 Wi-Fi TX (seen at the 5 V rail through the LDO), with the receiver and RTC adding under 30 mA. A 2 A adapter covers it, but the 1000 µF bulk capacitor is what actually prevents a brownout during the simultaneous Wi-Fi-TX-plus-chime moment. For a hardwired build, an HLK-5M05 isolated 5 V AC-DC module is the internal option — mains work, electrician only.

Firmware Note (Out of Scope Here)

NTP is the primary time source (SNTP against pool.ntp.org), the RTC is the fallback when the WAN is unplugged. EV1527 is a 24-bit ASK/OOK code with a sync pulse — decode the receiver's DATA pulses by timing in firmware. MP3 needs a decoder library (e.g. ESP32-audioI2S) and benefits from the 8 MB PSRAM; WAV playback is the simplest path for the first build.

Procurement Notes

Speaker: neither Robu.in nor Robocraze has a verified in-stock 4 Ω / 3 W driver, so no link is supplied — buy it from a local component shop and measure 3–4 Ω with the multimeter before wiring. Transmitter: the in-stock Robu.in unit is specified as fixed-code, not explicitly EV1527; confirm decoding against the RXB6 on the bench before treating it as the outdoor bell push. Adapter: the Pro-Range listing does not state BIS/ISI; check the mark on the unit you receive. All other rows were confirmed “In Stock” at the linked product page with the price shown.

Two Procurement Traps

1. The kinetic button must actually be EV1527 on 433.92 MHz. Several battery-free bells use a different code or 868 MHz. Ask the seller for the encoding before paying; a wrong button means the receiver will never decode it. 2. DS3231 ZS-042 boards often include a trickle-charge circuit for LIR2032 cells. Fitting a non-rechargeable CR2032 on such a board shortens its life — remove the charge resistor/diode or use the specified rechargeable cell.

3 — Pre-Power-On Verification Checklist

Run every check with the adapter unplugged and the ESP32 USB cable disconnected. A multimeter in continuity/beep mode and in DC volts mode is sufficient. Do not proceed past a failed row.

First-Power Gate

Only after all twelve rows pass, connect the ESP32 USB cable and flash firmware. Confirm the bell with a single quiet WAV first, then exercise the NTP clock, the RTC fallback with the WAN cable unplugged, and finally a scheduled reminder.
