Always-On Wi-Fi Calling Bell & Talking Clock

A commercial, ultra-low-cost, always-on smart calling bell and talking clock for the Indian market: 220 V AC mains powered, strictly local-only, with a battery-backed RTC that keeps accurate date and time through power cuts, a 433 MHz EV1527 bell-push trigger, custom chimes, and a local web UI served from flash. The production target is a 2-layer, ~50 × 50 mm PCB built turnkey at LionCircuits (India) at roughly ₹540 ex-GST per unit for a 5-unit batch. This page is a standalone hardware guide — it deliberately sits outside the five-section Thread/Matter navigation chain so the main wiki's strict reading order stays intact.

Scope & Conventions

This is a separate product document, not a new section of the Apartment Thread blueprint. It reuses the same shell, badges, tables, and verification-gate conventions as the rest of the wiki. The stack below is locked: no alternative MCUs, RTCs, amplifier ICs, external DACs, or cloud services are proposed. Pin numbers, values, and cost targets are engineering inputs for the schematic/PCBA release, not verified retail buy links.

Locked Stack & Electrical Hazards — Read Before Any Work

Locked parts: ESP-12F (ESP8266, 4 MB flash), PCF8563 RTC + 32.768 kHz crystal + CR2032, PAM8403 Class-D amp with a DAC-free 1-bit Delta-Sigma audio path, RXB12/RXB6 433.92 MHz receiver, AMS1117-3.3 LDO, and a bare 5 V 1 A isolated SMPS. No alternative MCU, RTC, external DAC, or switch technology is permitted. Mains: 220 V AC is present on the SMPS daughterboard — maintain HV/LV creepage and clearance with isolation slots, and let an electrician handle all mains wiring. PAM8403: its BTL outputs must never be tied together or to ground; the 4 Ω speaker connects only between OUT+ and OUT−. GPIO3: it is the ESP-12F UART RX pin during flashing and is also the audio data output — keep the audio path disconnected/muted during the one-time factory USB-UART flash and use OTA for all later updates.

1 — Circuit & Pin-to-Pin Wiring Guide

Bare castellated ESP-12F module on the PCB, with all core ICs as SMD parts for automated assembly. Only the specialty items — the 433 MHz receiver, 5 V SMPS daughterboard, CR2032 holder, speaker, antenna wire, and flashing header — use through-hole pads for consignment/manual insertion.

Why the DAC-Free Path and a 5 V / 1 A Supply

PAM8403 delivers about 3 W into 4 Ω from the 5 V rail, so the amp is powered directly from the isolated SMPS. The ESP-12F generates audio with a 1-bit Delta-Sigma stream on GPIO3; a single 1 kΩ/0.1 µF RC network is enough to reconstruct it for the amp input, which removes the cost of an external I2S DAC. The AMS1117-3.3 drops 5 V to 3.3 V for the ESP-12F, PCF8563, and RXB12. Keep bulk capacitance on the 5 V rail so an ESP8266 Wi-Fi TX burst does not sag the amp supply.

Firmware & Audio (delivered in Phases 3–4)

Lean C/C++ on the Arduino ESP8266 core or ESP8266 RTOS SDK, with a LittleFS partition on the 4 MB flash. Talking-clock clips are stitched and stored at 8 kHz 8-bit mono (about 200 KB total), preserving roughly 3 MB for custom chimes and UI assets. The local web UI (Vanilla HTML/JS served from LittleFS, no CDN) sets RTC date/time from a phone browser clock, uploads/deletes bell audio, pairs an EV1527 24-bit transmitter ID, configures hourly/scheduled announcements and quiet hours, and triggers OTA updates. For real-time stability, detach the GPIO14 interrupt during playback and re-attach afterwards, defer HTTP handling while audio plays, and use the watchdog for 24/7 uptime.

Manufacturing & Layout Notes

The ₹540 figure is a small-batch target, not a quoted price — lock it with a LionCircuits quote before release. The 2-layer stackup must keep a clear antenna keep-out zone around the ESP-12F and route the 17.3 cm receiver antenna away from the SMPS. Maintain HV/LV creepage and clearance with isolation slots between the 220 V AC section and all low-voltage traces. In the BOM CSV, keep turnkey SMD parts and consigned through-hole/daughterboard parts in separate columns so LionCircuits can quote assembly correctly.

3 — Pre-Power-On Verification Checklist

Run every low-voltage check with the mains unplugged and the factory USB-UART cable disconnected. A multimeter in continuity/beep mode and DC volts mode is sufficient. Mains-side checks are electrician-only. Do not proceed past a failed row.

First-Power Gate

Only after all twelve rows pass, perform the one-time factory USB-UART flash through the 4-pin header (audio path disconnected), then reconnect the speaker and test a single quiet chime. Set the RTC from a phone browser, pair an EV1527 transmitter ID, test scheduled announcements and quiet hours, and apply firmware updates only through OTA.
