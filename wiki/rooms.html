Room Automation Patterns

Three rooms, three distinct automation patterns. We identify reusable architectural patterns for Indian apartments: timer-driven hall lighting without sensors, mmWave presence-driven study light and fan, and door contact-triggered bathroom lighting and ventilation.

1. The Hall Pattern — Timer-Driven Lighting (No Sensor)

Pattern Philosophy — Zero Sensor Cost

Hallways and living rooms have high transient foot traffic, wide open sightlines, and roaming pets where radar sensors often create nuisance triggers and drain batteries. No presence sensor is required. Instead, deterministic time/schedule automations trigger the Tinxy switch to turn the light on and off, with the physical wall switch always ready for manual use.

Automation Rules & Schedule

  • Evening Schedule: 15 minutes before sunset or at 18:30 → Turn ON switch.hall_light automatically.
  • Bedtime Schedule: At 23:00 → Turn OFF switch.hall_light automatically.
  • Manual Override: Outside scheduled hours, the occupant simply taps the physical wall switch on the Tinxy plate.

2. The Study Room Pattern — Presence-Driven Light & Fan

Pattern Philosophy — Stationary Occupant Detection

In a study room or home office, people sit stationary for long hours reading, typing, or on video calls. Traditional PIR sensors fail here because they only detect bulk motion, plunging the occupant into darkness. The Sentinel mmWave presence sensor (PS-1) detects micro-movements (breathing, typing). Presence turns both the light and the fan ON; vacating the room turns both OFF.

Automation Rules & Hold Logic

  • Occupancy Trigger: Sentinel mmWave radar (PS-1) detects micro-movement (breathing/typing) → turns ON both switch.study_light and switch.study_fan within 1 second.
  • Vacancy Delay: When the occupant leaves, the radar clears (off) and a 2-minute vacancy buffer (00:02:00) begins. Brief micro-shadows or momentary step-outs do not prematurely kill light and fan.
  • Hold Expiration: Once 120 seconds of continuous zero-occupancy elapse, both light and fan turn OFF automatically.

3. The Bathroom Pattern — Hardwired Door Contact State Machine

Pattern Philosophy — Physical Boundary Determinism & The Hardwired Dummy Channel

Bathrooms possess an absolute physical boundary: the door. In standard Indian apartments, the switchboard is positioned directly adjacent to the door frame. Rather than relying on battery-powered wireless links or central hub polling, the hardwired door contact sensor (DS-1) connects directly to the switchboard as a "Dummy Channel" (switch input S2 on the Tinxy module). An on-chip state machine executes locally on the microcontroller silicon with zero hub dependency, while exposing full bidirectional state to Home Assistant when a server is present.

The Automation State Machine — Solving Entry & Exit Locally

A naive rule like "Door closed → Light ON; Door open → Light OFF" fails when someone enters: they open the door, step in, and are left in pitch darkness until they pull the door shut! Similarly, a blind "Toggle on close" rule desynchronizes if someone opens and shuts the door without staying inside. The on-chip state machine evaluates conditional light state to resolve all 4 states cleanly with sub-5ms local response:

Bathroom Presence State Machine (On-Chip Execution)
  1. State 1 — Entry Trigger (Door Transitions CLOSED → OPEN, Light currently OFF): Light (switch.bathroom_light) turns ON immediately (<5ms). The threshold illuminates before the occupant steps into the room. A 60-second entry timer starts locally on silicon.
  2. State 2 — Occupied Latch (Door Transitions OPEN → CLOSED): The occupant is inside. The entry countdown is cancelled immediately. Light and exhaust fan (fan.bathroom_exhaust_fan) lock ON continuously. No motion dropout can ever leave a showering occupant in the dark.
  3. State 3 — Exit Trigger (Door Transitions CLOSED → OPEN, Light ALREADY ON): The occupant finishes and opens the door to exit. A 60-second vacancy countdown begins on the chip's internal clock.
  4. State 4 — Vacated (Timer Expires OR Door Re-closed during Exit): Light and fan turn OFF automatically.
  5. 45-Minute Safety Watchdog: If the door is accidentally shut while vacant, the on-chip timer forces the load off after 45 minutes to prevent wasted electricity.

Standalone Hardware Execution & HASS Integration

  • Standalone Mode (No Server / Hub): For deployments without a central Home Assistant server, the ESP8266 microcontroller inside the switch module executes the entire conditional logic, timer tracking (via millis()), and load switching autonomously on silicon.
  • Central HASS Mode: When Home Assistant is active on the local network, the module auto-discovers via native ESPHome API. HASS can read door/light state, trigger manual overrides from dashboards, and adjust timer parameters without breaking the local on-chip fallback.

Wiring & Sensor Placement Guidelines

  1. Exterior Door Frame Placement: Mount the passive magnetic reed switch on the door frame header or strike jamb, routed via thin concealed 2-core wire directly into the adjacent switchboard box.
  2. Gap Tolerance: Ensure the alignment gap between the magnet on the moving door leaf and the reed sensor is ≤15 mm when latched shut.
  3. Steam & Condensation Protection: Mounting the dry contact on the exterior door jamb keeps electrical contacts isolated from hot shower steam and condensation pools.

The Wall Switch Always Wins

If Home Assistant, Wi-Fi, Thread, or the Radxa SBC is offline, every light and fan must still operate instantaneously from the physical wall switches. The Tinxy 4+1 Smart Switch plate preserves full physical switch mechanical/relay control independently of microcontroller logic (O-18).
