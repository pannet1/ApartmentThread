Door Contact Sensor

The low-latency physical boundary sensor. Reports instantaneous open/close events to Home Assistant to trigger room illumination before an occupant steps across the threshold. Evaluated with 100% unified Matter over Thread, turnkey Zigbee, and wired dry-contact options.

Component Identity — DS-1

Matter-over-Thread Door Contact Sensor (Option 1): Built on the Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32-C6 microcontroller with an MC-38 magnetic reed switch and 2,000 mAh LiPo battery. Operates as a Thread Sleepy End Device (SED), sharing the solitary M5Stack ESP32-H2 Thread Border Router with the Study Room presence sensor. For physical bench assembly, wiring, and Matter pairing, see the dedicated Stage 3b · Door Sensor Build Guide →. For the automation state machine and exhaust fan rules driven by this sensor, see Bathroom Pattern.

Engineering Analysis: Latency & Electrical Safety

A door sensor seems trivial until deployed for room lighting. In rooms where occupants enter in the dark, poor sensor selection leads to instant usability failure or severe electrical hazards.

1. The Latency Trap — Why Battery Wi-Fi Sensors Fail for Lighting

Never use battery-powered Wi-Fi door sensors (Tuya Wi-Fi, Sonoff DW2 Wi-Fi) for lighting automation:
• The Cold Boot RF Sequence: To preserve battery, a Wi-Fi sensor turns off its 2.4 GHz radio in deep sleep. On door opening, the microcontroller wakes up → powers RF synthesizer → scans channels (800–1,500 ms) → associates with Wi-Fi AP → performs WPA2 handshake (600–1,000 ms) → requests DHCP IP lease (500–1,200 ms) → opens TCP socket and publishes MQTT state (300–500 ms).
• Total Latency: 2.5 to 5.0 seconds! The occupant opens the door at night, walks into pitch darkness, and waits 3 seconds before the light turns on. Human reaction threshold is <300 ms. By 1,000 ms, occupants conclude automation failed and jab the physical wall switch.
• Rapid Battery Death: High-power Wi-Fi wakes draw 80–150 mA for 3 seconds. Coin-cell or AAA batteries are exhausted in 6 to 8 weeks.

2. The Mains Isolation Barrier — Switchboard vs Door Frame

Never connect a wired reed switch directly to the Tinxy 4+1 switch plate terminals:
• The Tinxy 4+1 Smart Switch is a 230V AC mains-powered modular unit. Its physical switch inputs operate at line voltage (Phase 230V).
• Running thin 2-core wire from an unisolated 230V AC switchboard terminal to a magnetic reed switch mounted on a metal door frame creates a lethal shock hazard (especially in wet bathroom areas) and directly violates Indian Electrical Standard IS 732.
• Strict Rule: Any wired magnetic dry contact (e.g., MC-38) must terminate only at an isolated, low-voltage 3.3V DC GPIO (such as the Radxa Zero 3E 40-pin GPIO header), never inside a mains switchboard.

Unifying with Matter over Thread: The "Twin Sensor" Approach

Architectural Harmony — 100% Matter over Thread

Adopting Matter over Thread for the door contact sensor creates the cleanest possible architecture:
• Single Border Router: The M5Stack ESP32-H2 serves as the solitary OpenThread Border Router (OTBR) for your entire apartment.
• Zero Protocol Fragmentation: Eliminates the need for a separate Zigbee coordinator (ZHA / Zigbee2MQTT). Every sensor in the house communicates via native IPv6 Thread packets directly into Home Assistant's Matter integration.
• Unified Commissioning: Both the presence sensor and the door sensor are paired using standard Matter QR codes.

Option 1: How to Build the DIY Matter-over-Thread Contact Sensor

You can build a battery-powered Matter-over-Thread door sensor using the exact same miniature form-factor as the Sentinel presence sensor:
• Microcontroller: Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32-C6 → (₹672 on Robocraze) or Seeed Studio XIAO BLE nRF52840 → (₹1,149). Both feature onboard 802.15.4 Thread radios and integrated LiPo battery chargers.
• Contact Element: An MC-38 Magnetic Reed Switch → (₹57) connected between a digital GPIO pin (Pin D1 / GPIO2) and GND with an internal pull-up resistor.
• Power Source: A single-cell Witty Fox 3.7V 2,000 mAh Prismatic Battery → (₹256 on Robocraze) with built-in protection PCB, soldered to underside pads (BAT+ and BAT-).
• Sleepy End Device (SED) Power Profile: Unlike the presence sensor which powers a 24 GHz radar, a door contact has zero active sensing draw. The MCU remains in deep sleep drawing <5 μA. On door movement, the reed switch generates a hardware GPIO interrupt, wakes the chip in <15 ms, transmits the Matter BooleanState report over Thread in <35 ms, and returns to deep sleep. A 2,000 mAh battery will last 4 to 5+ years.
• Total DIY Build Cost: ₹672 (XIAO C6) + ₹57 (MC-38) + ₹256 (Battery) = ₹985.
