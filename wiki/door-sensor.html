Door Contact Sensor

The low-latency physical boundary sensor. Reports instantaneous open/close events to Home Assistant to trigger room illumination before an occupant steps across the threshold. Evaluated with 100% unified Matter over Thread, turnkey Zigbee, and wired dry-contact options.

Component Identity — DS-1

Hardwired "Dummy Channel" Door Sensor (Option 1 — Default Build): A standard MC-38 magnetic reed switch (₹57) installed on the bathroom door frame and hardwired directly into the adjacent switchboard box to switch input S2 of the Tinxy module. The module's ESP8266 microcontroller runs an autonomous on-chip state machine via ESPHome, delivering sub-5ms local response, zero batteries, and 100% offline standalone operation with no hub required. When Home Assistant is present, it auto-integrates for remote monitoring and parameter adjustments. For physical bench assembly, wiring, and flashing, see the dedicated Stage 3b · Door Sensor Build Guide →. For the automation state machine and exhaust fan rules driven by this sensor, see Bathroom Pattern.

Engineering Analysis: The Hardwired Dummy Channel vs Wireless Alternatives

A door sensor seems trivial until deployed for room lighting. In Indian bathrooms where switchboards are almost universally placed on the wall immediately adjacent to the door frame, physical wiring can bridge the gap for under ₹100, outperforming wireless sensors in latency, reliability, and maintenance.

The Hardwired "Dummy Channel" Architecture

How It Operates on the Silicon:
• The Physical Loop: An inexpensive wired magnetic reed switch (MC-38, ₹57) mounts on the door frame. A thin, concealed two-core cable routes directly into the adjacent bathroom switchboard.
• Channel Assignment: On a Tinxy 2-node (or 4-node) module, Channel 1 is connected to the bathroom light (and exhaust fan). Channel 2 has no electrical load (bulb) connected to its relay output—it functions as the dedicated "Dummy Channel."
• The Switch Input (S2): The two wires from the door reed switch connect across switch input S2. When the door closes, the contact completes; when the door opens, the contact breaks.
• Autonomous On-Chip State Machine: Running custom ESPHome firmware, the ESP8266 detects S2 state transitions directly on silicon (<5ms hardware interrupt). It evaluates whether the light was already ON to distinguish entry from exit, manages entry/exit timers using internal hardware clocks (millis()), and executes watchdog auto-off—operating completely standalone without a central server or Wi-Fi router.
• Home Assistant Bidirectional Control: If a local Home Assistant server is deployed, the module exposes both the light switch and door binary sensor natively over the local LAN for dashboard control and timer slider tuning.

Mains Safety & Wiring Guidelines for Dummy Channel

Electrical Precautions for In-Wall Switchboard Sensing:
• Non-Isolated Input Plane: In retrofit modules, the switch inputs (S1, S2) share the internal power reference plane. Always use mains-rated 0.5–0.75 mm² insulated wire (1,100V grade) inside sealed PVC conduit or casing when running the contact wires from the switchboard to the door frame.
• Enclosed Contact Housing: Use an enclosed reed switch capsule (such as the screw-mount MC-38 with sealed ABS housing) mounted on the exterior door frame jamb away from direct shower spray or standing condensation pools.

Why Battery Wi-Fi Sensors Fail for Lighting

Never use battery-powered Wi-Fi door sensors (Tuya Wi-Fi, Sonoff DW2 Wi-Fi) for lighting automation:
• The Cold Boot RF Sequence: Wi-Fi sensors take 2.5 to 5.0 seconds to wake from deep sleep, associate with an AP, and negotiate DHCP/MQTT. The occupant walks into pitch darkness.
• Rapid Battery Drain: High-power Wi-Fi wake bursts exhaust coin cells within 6 to 8 weeks.

Alternative Architecture: Wireless Matter over Thread (Option 2)

For Layouts Without Adjacent Switchboards

If the bathroom switchboard is far from the door frame or conduit cannot be run, adopt the Matter over Thread DIY Contact Sensor (Option 2):
• Microcontroller: Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32-C6 → (₹672) with onboard 802.15.4 Thread radio.
• Power & Life: 2,000 mAh LiPo battery running as a Sleepy End Device (<5μA draw, 4–5 year life).
• Mesh: Joins the solitary M5Stack ESP32-H2 Thread Border Router alongside the Study room presence sensor.

Option 1: How to Build the DIY Matter-over-Thread Contact Sensor

You can build a battery-powered Matter-over-Thread door sensor using the exact same miniature form-factor as the Sentinel presence sensor:
• Microcontroller: Seeed Studio XIAO ESP32-C6 → (₹672 on Robocraze) or Seeed Studio XIAO BLE nRF52840 → (₹1,149). Both feature onboard 802.15.4 Thread radios and integrated LiPo battery chargers.
• Contact Element: An MC-38 Magnetic Reed Switch → (₹57) connected between a digital GPIO pin (Pin D1 / GPIO2) and GND with an internal pull-up resistor.
• Power Source: A single-cell Witty Fox 3.7V 2,000 mAh Prismatic Battery → (₹256 on Robocraze) with built-in protection PCB, soldered to underside pads (BAT+ and BAT-).
• Sleepy End Device (SED) Power Profile: Unlike the presence sensor which powers a 24 GHz radar, a door contact has zero active sensing draw. The MCU remains in deep sleep drawing <5 μA. On door movement, the reed switch generates a hardware GPIO interrupt, wakes the chip in <15 ms, transmits the Matter BooleanState report over Thread in <35 ms, and returns to deep sleep. A 2,000 mAh battery will last 4 to 5+ years.
• Total DIY Build Cost: ₹672 (XIAO C6) + ₹57 (MC-38) + ₹256 (Battery) = ₹985.
